# FlyLab numeric library provenance audit (round 2)

Scope: 46 non-null `value_M` receptor rows in the baseline `source/flylab/pharm/library.yaml`, read on 23 September 2026. The accompanying CSV records each row's baseline source, a verified or candidate identifier, evidence type, and a specific action. Seven values were subsequently withheld and two fipronil rows reclassified. The audit separates a missing DOI/PMID string from insufficient support for a numeric constant. A source's existence or a matching DOI does not establish its assay result.

**Audit baseline:** The CSV preserves the original 46-row findings for traceability. The current library and generated T10 contain the subsequent corrections.

## Immediate corrections before mechanistic claims

1. **Spinosad (`insect_nAChR` and `insect_nAChR_alpha6`).** The cited [Perry et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25747008/) establishes Dα6 involvement through in-vivo rescue, but says robust heterologous expression was absent. It does not substantiate a 5 µM receptor EC50. A later [Drosophila study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35191376/) reports low-dose spinosad antagonizing α6-mediated cholinergic response, so the agonist direction also needs reconsideration. Do not simply append Perry's DOI to these rows.
2. **Ivermectin (`insect_RDL`).** The cited [Lees et al. 2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0097468) directly contradicts `positive_modulator`: ivermectin suppressed GABA-evoked RDL currents, with no current when 10 µM ivermectin was applied alone. For RDLbd I/V it reports pIC50 6.33 (about 0.47 µM), an inhibitory parameter. The library's 1 µM *EC50* must be reviewed in the model as well as the source field.
3. **Fipronil (`vertebrate_GABA_A`).** The 1103 nM number is in [Hainzl, Cole and Casida 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9860498/), averaged over several vertebrates by radioligand displacement. The current source attributes it to a human recombinant receptor in Ratra and Casida 2001. The number can be retained only with the proper assay and species scope.
4. **Permethrin (`vertebrate_Nav1_x`).** The cited [Smith and Soderlund 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9863771/) concerns *cypermethrin*, not permethrin. It does not support the 40 µM permethrin row.
5. **DDT (`insect_Nav`).** [Burton et al. 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21640822/) directly supports 65 µM tail-current EC50 but reports only 7% maximum channel modification. This low efficacy must accompany any resulting mechanistic or selectivity claim.

Other specific corrections: the Watson 2011 sulfoxaflor paper is [*Insect Biochem. Mol. Biol.* 41:432–439](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21296156/), not *Pestic. Biochem. Physiol.* 101:32; 154 µM remains unconfirmed. The Tan and Soderlund 2010 rat Nav1.6 study is [*Toxicol. Appl. Pharmacol.* 247:229–237](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2929565/), not *Neurotoxicology* 31:16; its exact 5 µM model value remains unconfirmed. [Fournier and Mutero 1994](https://hero.epa.gov/reference/4491250/) is a resistance review, not a primary 10 nM chlorpyrifos-oxon assay. [Kasteel et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33037899/) does directly support the *human-blood* chlorpyrifos-oxon IC50 of 0.27 µM (rounded to 0.3 µM in the library).

The 43 numeric rows without literal DOI/PMID in their source strings should receive an identifier only after the source and its assay are matched. Class-order placeholder and cross-receptor rows should be labeled as model assumptions; identifier absence alone is not reason for E4 demotion. The CSV's “value unconfirmed” means the paper identity or abstract was checked but the exact table or figure was not, so it is an audit lead rather than a claim of falsity.
