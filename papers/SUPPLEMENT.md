# Supplementary material

**Supplement to:** *Typed Pharmacological Evidence and Connectome-Dependence Testing: A Computational Method for Drug Simulation on a Synapse-Resolution* Drosophila *Connectome*.

FlyLab 0.5.0, commit `c4a0011d9562`, library SHA-256 `e52251389c0c`, map `male-cns:v1.0`. Reference numbers `[n]` refer to the manuscript's reference list. Every number here is generated by `scripts/reproduce_paper.py` from committed artifacts; none was measured in a living animal.

## S1. Mechanism rules: the gain patch and its rationale

`flylab/pharm/mechanisms.py` is the single source of truth mapping engagement rows onto circuit gains; no assay carries its own copy, which is what makes Table T2 a truthful description of the software. Gains are dimensionless multipliers on the synaptic weight of a transmitter class (or, for `g_nav`, on node excitability); 1.0 is vehicle, and every gain is floored at 0.05 because a network with a gain of exactly zero is not informative. The 15 rules are listed in full in T2. The four shipped since v0.4 may not be changed silently:

* **Insect nAChR, activating:** `g_ach = max(0.05, 1 + 0.4θ − 1.6θ²)`. Rises at low engagement and falls at high engagement, encoding excitation followed by desensitisation and depolarisation block. At θ = 0.99 it is at the floor, which is why a saturating dose of a nicotinic agonist silences cholinergic transmission in the model rather than merely reducing it — and, as §3.4 of the manuscript shows, why the suppression conclusion is a property of *this* rule.
* **Insect nAChR, blocking:** `g_ach = max(0.05, 1 − θ)`.
* **Insect RDL, blocking:** `g_gaba = max(0.05, 1 − θ)`. Blocking the GABA-gated chloride channel removes inhibition.
* **Insect RDL, activating:** `g_gaba = max(0.05, 1 + 0.4θ)`.

Four families added in v0.5 share the shape and state a rationale each: GluCl activation uses a larger coefficient (`1 + 0.8θ`) than RDL because avermectin opening is effectively irreversible [38]; AChE inhibition raises an ambient cholinergic tone (`ach_tone = 1 + 2.0θ`) multiplying `g_ach` together with the agonist curve, reproducing the organophosphate excitation-then-block picture; Nav modulation scales node excitability globally (`g_nav = 1 + 1.5θ`), since pyrethroids and DDT act on every cell regardless of transmitter [23], [67]; octopamine agonism uses the smallest coefficient (`1 + 0.5θ`) because it is modulatory.

**These coefficients and shapes are modelling choices, not measurements.** That is why the manuscript prespecifies a family of 25 alternatives (§2.4) and re-derives every conclusion under each, and why the variance analysis nominates this transformation as the assumption worth measuring first.

## S2. Null-model definitions

`flylab/analysis/nullmodels.py` degrades the same committed cut in four ways, in increasing order of destruction:

* **`sign_permute`** — consensus transmitter labels are permuted across nodes. The transmitter histogram, and therefore the global excitation/inhibition balance, is preserved *exactly*; which cell is cholinergic is destroyed.
* **`weight_permute`** — synapse counts are permuted across edges. Topology and transmitters are kept; the pairing between wiring and strength is destroyed.
* **`rewire_degree_preserving`** — double-edge swaps, roughly 10 × |E| accepted swaps. Every node keeps its in-degree, out-degree and transmitter; the wiring pattern is destroyed.
* **`erdos_renyi`** — the same node and edge counts, endpoints drawn uniformly, weights drawn from the empirical weight list. Only the size of the graph and its census survive.

Every mode preserves the node set, the node order and the seed block — the same named cells are driven and read in the shuffled graph — and never creates self-loops or duplicate edges. The effect is always `treated − vehicle` with **both arms on the same graph**, so a shuffle can change it only through the wiring. The reported probability is the empirical two-sided permutation *p*, `(k+1)/(n+1)`, with resolution `1/(n+1)`; on the landscape the same `n` shuffled graphs are reused by every cell (a paired design), and shuffle *i* depends only on `(seed, i)`, so each cell's numbers are identical to running its profile alone.

**Permutation counts.** Manuscript profiles: 1000 permutations per mode on the `named` cut (resolution 0.0010), 300 on the `taste_motor` cut, 100 for each of the 84 landscape cells. The sweep (T11) shows the verdict at α = 0.05 settling from *n* ≈ 50–400, and a mid-range *p* reaching ±0.02 of its final value between *n* = 25 and 400.

**Why `z` is secondary.** A null distribution built on degraded graphs is not normal, and for the Erdős–Rényi mode its variance can collapse, which produces numerically enormous *z* values that mean only "decisively different". The manuscript quotes *p* first for this reason.

## S3. Runtimes and their calibration

**Rate runtime.** `flylab/circuit/rate.py` holds a dense signed weight matrix `W_signed[j,i] = sign(nt_i) × synapses(i→j)`, with +1 for acetylcholine, −1 for GABA and glutamate (the insect glutamatergic sign convention at GluCl-bearing targets) and 0 for cells with no consensus prediction. A gain patch scales the columns by the presynaptic transmitter's gain. The update is a clipped leaky iteration `r ← clip((1 − α)·r + α·g_nav·(d + W_eff·r), 0, r_max)` with α = 0.3, 80 steps, `r_max` = 300 Hz and *d* the per-node external drive in Hz. It is deterministic, and one evaluation of the 1126-node graph costs about 50 ms with single-threaded BLAS — which is what makes 1000-permutation profiles and an 84-cell landscape affordable at all. Pinning BLAS to one thread matters: the matrices are small enough that the thread pool costs more than it saves, and the pipeline sets `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` and its siblings before importing numpy.

**LIF runtime.** `flylab/circuit/lif.py` is a current-based leaky integrate-and-fire network with the parameters of Shiu *et al.* [7]: τ_m = 20 ms, V_rest = −52 mV, V_th = −45 mV, V_reset = −52 mV, refractory 2.2 ms, exponential synaptic current with τ_s = 5 ms, forward Euler at dt = 0.1 ms. A presynaptic spike on edge *i*→*j* injects `w_scale × synapses × sign(nt_i) × gain(nt_i)` mV into *j*.

Two choices are modelling decisions, not literature values, and both are stated because they set every absolute LIF number in the paper.

*The synaptic scale is a one-time calibration scan.* `w_scale` = 0.05 mV per synapse and a drive weight of 0.5 mV per event were chosen once, by scanning, so that vehicle MN9 cells fire in a plausible band under the standard 150 Hz seed drive and the rest of the cut is neither silent nor saturated. No recording was used and no error was minimised against data, so this does not consume the one fitted parameter the project allows — but it is a tuned constant and should be read as one.

*A background Poisson drive is required, and it is a choice.* Excising a 1-hop neighbourhood removes every input from outside it; without an external source most cells never spike and the drug cannot act on them. Every non-seed cell therefore receives an independent Poisson drive at 0.65 × the seed rate, standing in for the amputated inputs. This is the most consequential free choice in the LIF arm: it sets the operating point and it is why the two engines report sugar-driven MN9 at 0.293 Hz (rate) against 105 Hz (LIF). The engines agree on **direction only**, and the manuscript does not present their agreement as cross-model validation.

## S4. Supporting capabilities

These are implemented, tested and used, but are supporting capabilities rather than contributions of this paper.

**Exposure.** `flylab/pharm/exposure.py` maps a dose and route onto *C(t)* and hence engagement(t) in one compartment. Its constants are class placeholders chosen so that the three routes differ in the direction the literature describes, and are fitted to no *Drosophila* ADME dataset because that dataset does not exist: of the seven quantities such a model needs, `data/literature/fly_pharmacokinetics.yaml` records only metabolite identity as well established [52], [54], [55], [58]. Adult haemolymph volume, adult body mass, cuticular penetration rate and any *Drosophila* elimination half-life are `null`; what is recorded is 25 nL recoverable per adult [57], 174 ± 81 nL in third-instar larvae [56], a water-partitioning measurement [53], an ethanol entry that could not supply a rate [65], and an imidacloprid half-life of 4.5–5 h in *honeybee* [58] — the only verified insect half-life for a library compound. Two structural warnings travel with every profile: haemolymph is the lowest-radioactivity compartment for imidacloprid, so a one-compartment haemolymph model under-estimates CNS exposure [59]; and a substantial metabolic route runs through the gut microbiome, so a single-enzyme clearance term is wrong for this compound [54].

**Genotype.** `flylab/pharm/genotype.py` applies published target-site resistance alleles as multiplicative potency shifts from `data/literature/resistance_alleles.yaml` [13]–[25], enforcing one rule: **a shift applies per compound, never per receptor.** Two published findings make the naive implementation wrong — *Rdl* A301S shifts GABA potency 33.9-fold but fipronil only 1.1-fold on the expressed planthopper receptor [15], and *para* M918T shifts deltamethrin 100-fold while leaving DDT at 1-fold [22]. An allele/compound pair with no sourced number is left unchanged and listed under `unshifted`, never extrapolated. One row is deliberately ugly: *Rdl* A301G in *D. simulans* carries a 20000-fold shift derived from a whole-animal resistance ratio, which drives fipronil engagement to 4.63e-04 and is almost certainly an overstatement of the target-site change [19]. The notebook says so. Panel: T9, F9.

**Mixtures.** `flylab/pharm/mixtures.py` implements Bliss independence [45] and Loewe additivity [48], [49] as explicit null models, following the response-surface framing of Greco *et al.* [46], and selects between them per pair: Loewe where the components share a receptor key, Bliss where their targets are disjoint. See the manuscript §3.6 for why the three-case comparison against the honeybee dataset [51] is a qualitative concordance check and not validation. Table T5.

**Expression weighting.** `flylab/pharm/expression.py` relaxes the assumption that every cell expresses the receptor, using class-level statements from single-cell atlases [39]–[44] mapped by hand onto MaleCNS superclasses, and interpolates each node's gain between 1.0 and the full scalar gain. It is a **sensitivity layer and never the primary model**: none of the cited atlases uses MaleCNS superclass labels, so every mapping row is tagged `cross_atlas_inference`; coverage is 0.205 of cells; and receptor expression in identified adult motor neurons is a confirmed gap, so MN9 carries the `unknown` default. Weighting shifts MN9 by -2.622 Hz at 1 µM imidacloprid, which we report as the size of the uncertainty rather than as a correction.

**Model dose-response.** `flylab/assays/ensemble.py` builds the model's own concentration-response curve, fits a four-parameter Hill function by Nelder–Mead in log-concentration space and bootstraps it. For imidacloprid on the `named` cut the circuit midpoint is 34.7 nM with a bootstrap 95 % CI of 35.2 nM to 71.5 nM (r² = 0.9999). The interval does not bracket the point estimate, and that is reported rather than hidden: the bootstrap resamples replicates within each concentration, the lower plateau of the ladder is short, and the midpoint is therefore poorly identified — which is the same conclusion the global variance analysis reaches from the other direction. This is in any case the **model's** circuit midpoint, not a receptor or an animal IC50. The one-at-a-time tornado at 1 µM finds exactly zero span for ec50, hill_n — the engagement is saturated — which the global analysis (manuscript §3.5) confirms and quantifies.

## S5. Prospective predictions

The following 7 hypotheses are written before any experiment and labelled `software_prediction`; `live_result` is `null` in every row and stays null until a real table is imported by hand. They are **prospective, not pre-registered**: a mutable repository is not a registry, and the wording will change only if a tagged release is externally archived with a timestamp. Full table: T3.

* **H1** (receptor engagement). At 1 µM, imidacloprid insect nicotinic engagement ≥ 0.9 while vertebrate α4β2 stays ≤ 0.2. Predicted gap 0.854. Checkable against published expressed-receptor work rather than against flies.
* **H2** (PER, `protocols/per.md`). The same dose lowers cholinergic drive in the MN9/DNp01 neighbourhood: predicted change in network mean rate -6.145 Hz. The control rate this needs could not be sourced; the nearest substitute is 40 % responders at 1 h food deprivation rising to 68 % at 2 h in 463 wild-type flies [61], which also shows that deprivation time is a larger nuisance variable than most effects FlyLab would try to detect.
* **H3** (climbing, `protocols/climbing.md`). Fipronil at 1 µM disinhibits the neighbourhood: predicted change 0.896 Hz, opposite in sign to H2. Negative geotaxis is the best-served endpoint [60], [64], [66].
* **H4** (negative control). Diazepam engages vertebrate GABA-A and not insect RDL in this library, so it moves no fly readout: predicted effect 0.000. A non-zero live effect would falsify either the library row [17] or the assumption that the compound reaches the CNS unmetabolised.
* **H5** (within-animal control). Bitter input vetoes sugar-driven MN9 on the map-extracted path: predicted change -0.296 Hz relative to sugar alone, matching the published direction [7], [63].
* **H6**. Fipronil relieves the bitter veto on the map path: predicted rise in the veto ratio 0.413. The dependence analysis shows this readout is not distinguishable from its shuffled controls, so a live confirmation would support the pharmacology and **not** the wiring.
* **H7** (selectivity negative control). Picrotoxin perturbs the fly neighbourhood while its receptor selectivity index is ≈ 0: predicted change 0.561 Hz. Any insect-selectivity claim the tool makes must fail for picrotoxin, and picrotoxin's lack of insect-over-vertebrate selectivity is independently attested [31].

Rows where the model's own output disagrees with the prospective direction: none.

**On sample sizes.** Model-internal intervals (6 replicates of library jitter, drive jitter and the RNG seed) contain no biological variance, so the standardised effect sizes they imply are absurd. T3 therefore caps *d* at 1.0 — a conventionally large biological effect — before computing *n*, which yields 23 per group for every row. **This is an illustrative planning minimum, not a power calculation**: it means "what a large effect would need", it assumes independent flies where vial-based protocols violate that, and it must not be used to size a study.

## S6. Evidence typing: the full rules

| Parameter type | Engagement model | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `Kd`, `Ki` | `binding_occupancy` | equilibrium binding constant; `C^n/(K^n + C^n)` is physical fractional occupancy |
| `EC50`, `IC50`, `Kb` | `functional_engagement` | a functional potency under particular assay conditions; the Hill curve is a normalised response, not occupancy |
| `relative_potency`, `class_order`, `unknown` | `not_modelled` | no concentration scale of its own, or no number at all; returns N/A |

A row whose `relation` is `unsupported` is `not_modelled` whatever its parameter type. Asking for a binding occupancy from an EC50, or for any numeric engagement from a placeholder row, raises `EvidenceTypeError`; the library validator rejects a placeholder that carries a value and a non-placeholder that does not. The census of the shipped library (101 rows over 21 compounds and 14 receptor keys) is Table T10 and Figure F11; the library currently reports none validation problems.

Counts: 34 EC50, 10 IC50, 1 Kd, 56 unknown. Relations: 9 exact compound/receptor/species, 15 exact receptor in another species, 10 related receptor, 11 class extrapolation, 56 unsupported. Rows entitled to a binding-occupancy model: imidacloprid at insect_nAChR_beta1 (Kd 8.3e-11 M).

## S8. Model-versus-implementation warnings carried in every notebook

Every assay returns a notebook (schema 0.3) whose `warnings` list ships with the artefact rather than in a console message: hops-limited neighbourhood, predicted (not measured) transmitter signs, teaching-tier potency values, reduced-circuit substitution where it applies, engagement-is-not-occupancy, and the not-modelled reason for every N/A row. The `provenance` block records the FlyLab version, git commit, library SHA-256, map name and citation, RNG seed and platform. The library hash is what makes the honesty rules auditable: if a number changes, the hash changes, and a notebook produced before the change cannot be confused with one produced after.
