## Recommendation

**Major revision.**

I want to be clear at the outset that this is not a routine "major revision". The manuscript is unusually honest, the instrumentation is real, and the response to the previous round was substantive. But four of the paper's load-bearing results are, in my assessment, artefacts of the substrate or of a criterion that could not have returned another answer, and the abstract asserts, twice, something the shipped code contains an explicit comment refusing to say. Those are fixable, and the fixes are mostly re-runs plus rewriting — but they change what the paper is allowed to conclude, so they cannot be handled as minor revision.

I have deliberately not re-reported the eight items the editor told me are in flight. Where the same underlying mistake appears in *other* places, I say so and give the locations.

## Summary of the contribution, in my words

FlyLab compiles published receptor pharmacology into a typed table — each row recording what the source actually measured (`Kd`/`EC50`/`IC50`/…) and how far that source sits from the modelled compound-receptor-species triple — and lets those two facts decide whether a Hill transform may be applied at all, and under what name. Rows with no support return N/A rather than a small plausible number; 56 of 101 rows are refused. The surviving engagements are mapped, through an asserted table of mechanism rules, onto per-transmitter gain multipliers on a signed rate network built from two hops-limited cuts of MaleCNS v1.0.

Around that coupling the paper builds five instruments: a ladder of progressively degraded graphs against which each prediction is permutation-tested (and labelled by the weakest rung the test cannot tell from the real graph); an ablation over four pipeline layers scored by rank agreement with the full model; a re-derivation of every qualitative conclusion under 25 prespecified engagement-to-gain rules; a Sobol' variance budget converted into a ranked experiment list; and a machine-readable audit of the provenance chain behind one readout.

The results the paper draws from its own instruments are deflating on purpose: most predictions do not need the specific wiring; the ordering information lives in the mechanism rules and the transmitter composition, not the topology; and the paper's most quotable circuit result — that a nicotinic agonist suppresses activity — reverses under every monotone gain rule.

**My assessment of that contribution.** The scientific discipline is the real achievement and it is better than the norm in this literature. The *computing* contribution as framed is weaker than claimed, for three reasons developed below: the "type system" is a two-column lookup table plus a validator, the dependence ladder is a careful packaging of standard network nulls whose own validity is never evaluated, and the evaluation is broad rather than deep — six instruments applied once each, mostly to one compound pair on one 1126-node graph, with no ground-truth recovery, no power analysis and no comparison against an alternative.

## Does the computing contribution survive scrutiny?

### The type system

As implemented (`flylab/pharm/evidence.py`) this is: an enum of parameter types, an enum of source relations, a dict `ALLOWED` mapping parameter type → engagement model (lines 145-160), one special-case constant `BINDING_OCCUPANCY_RELATION` (line 161), a function `model_for` (line ~261) that composes the two, and an `EvidenceTypeError` raised on a disallowed request (lines 232, 248). There is no inference, no composition of types, no parametricity, and no soundness statement. A programming-languages or software-engineering referee will read this as a domain-specific taxonomy plus input validation, and will say so.

That is not a criticism of the *idea*, which is good and which changes results rather than documentation — refusing 56 of 101 rows is the single most useful thing in the paper. It is a criticism of the framing. Two ways to earn the word:

1. State and check a **soundness invariant** across the whole pipeline — e.g. "no numeric engagement reaching a circuit readout can originate from a row whose (`param_type`, `relation`) pair does not admit one" — and verify it by construction or by an exhaustive test over the library, not just at the call site. That would be a genuine, transferable computing contribution and would distinguish the work from validation.
2. Otherwise rename it ("evidence typing discipline", "provenance-gated transformation rules") and let the scientific value stand on its own.

There is also a structural problem arriving with the in-flight cross-species fix (see **I14**): after it, the `binding_occupancy` branch has **no instances** in the shipped library. A three-way type system one of whose branches is never taken is a two-way distinction with extra vocabulary.

### Connectome-dependence analysis

The individual nulls are standard: a configuration-model rewire, a weight permutation, a label permutation and an Erdős–Rényi control, scored by a two-sided permutation probability. None of that is new. What is potentially new is (a) ordering them by *retained information* and reporting the weakest rung the test cannot distinguish, and (b) doing it routinely, per prediction, rather than once per paper.

I think (b) is a defensible contribution and the paper is right to call it the transferable part. But three things stop me from crediting it as it stands:

- The ladder is **not monotone in 8 of the 20 positive cells** (I6), and the code knows — `dependence.py:719-731` emits a "the ladder is not monotone here" warning for exactly this case. None of it reaches the manuscript.
- The equivalence criterion that would let "the weakest rung that *suffices*" mean anything is only arriving now (editor's item 2). Until it lands, every rung label is a failure to reject.
- **The instrument is never evaluated.** There is no demonstration that it can detect topology dependence when topology dependence is present. That is a one-afternoon experiment: plant a known topology-dependent effect in a synthetic graph of the same size and density, and show the ladder recovers it. Without it, the flagship negative results (§3.2, §3.3) are indistinguishable from an under-powered test — and **B2** and **B3** give concrete reasons to expect exactly that.

### Is the evaluation deep enough for IJRC?

No — it is broad and shallow, and I say that as the main reason this is not a minor revision. Six instruments, each run once. The dependence profile at n = 1000 covers **two compounds on one graph**; the landscape covers 84 cells but at n = 100 with no effect-size floor (**I7**); the ablation is four levels at four concentrations with one of the four structurally unable to represent half the library (**I2**); the stability matrix is 25 specifications but its two topology conclusions were computed under a criterion that could not fail (**B4**); the Sobol' budget is one readout at one dose, with a misstated noise floor (**I5**).

What IJRC's "evaluate it in depth" would look like here: the dependence test characterised against known ground truth; the ladder's power as a function of *n*, graph density and effect size; the composition/topology split reproduced on the denser `taste_motor` cut and on at least one graph that is not a 1-hop star; and the ablation's ρ compared against what ρ would be under a null where the levels are known to share no information (**I3**).

## Questions to the authors

1. **B1.** Was the row normalisation in `rate.py:243-245` a deliberate modelling choice or an inherited implementation detail from `assays/subgraph.py`? Given that it makes each cell's input a composition-weighted average of presynaptic gains, on what basis can a test of composition-versus-topology on this engine's output be read as a statement about connectomes rather than about the engine?

2. **B2.** Do you agree that permuting node labels does not preserve the *weighted* excitation/inhibition balance, and that the real cut sits at the 99.9th percentile of its own sign-permutation null on cholinergic out-weight? If so, what is the justification for reading imidacloprid's *p* = 0.275 as "composition dominates" rather than "this null is too wide to say"?

3. **B3.** What fraction of the `named` cut's edges would you expect to terminate on the seed cells in a representative CNS subgraph? Do the paper's dependence conclusions hold on the `taste_motor` cut, where mean degree is 10.4 rather than 1.21?

4. **B4.** Were the T14 numbers in the manuscript produced at `stab_n_shuffles = 6`? If so, how do you interpret a 25/25 retention rate for C3 when *p* ≤ 0.05 was arithmetically unattainable? And what, after regeneration at n = 100, would constitute *evidence against* C3 — i.e. what would it look like for the topology conclusions not to be specification-independent?

5. **I3.** What value of ρ(B,D) would you have regarded as surprising? I could not find a stated expectation anywhere, and without one the ablation's headline is unfalsifiable.

6. **I5.** Why is the estimator's noise floor taken from the null factor (−0.007) rather than from the largest negative first-order estimate (`gain_coef`, −0.046)? Does `drive` (S1 = 0.019, CI −0.072 to 0.112) survive as a ranked factor under the latter definition?

7. **I7.** Is there a prespecified magnitude below which you would decline to classify a cell at all? `gaba` at 1e-7 M is classified topology-dependent on an effect of 2.7 × 10⁻⁵ Hz.

8. **I10/I9.** What is the provenance of `SIGN = {glutamate: −0.4, histamine: −0.5, dopamine: +0.2, serotonin: +0.2, octopamine: +0.2}`, and why are these excluded from the specification family when the nAChR gain coefficients are included? Apart from the specific errors listed above, **I could not find a number in the prose that does not trace to a generated value.** The template discipline (`.md.in` → `results.json` → rendered) is real and works; the numeric spans in the manuscript are all `{{key}}` substitutions. That is better than most submissions I review and the authors should keep it.

**Claims I specifically tried to break and could not:**

- **The rate/LIF agreement is never used as corroboration.** I searched for any place the two engines' agreement is offered as cross-model validation. §3.6 states it as a non-validation ("The two engines agree on **direction only** … This is not cross-model validation and we do not present it as such"), S3 repeats the reason (the background Poisson drive sets the operating point), and §4 lists it as caveat (iv). The 0.293 Hz vs 105 Hz discrepancy is quoted in the open rather than buried. Clean.
- **The tie/saturation explanation for the ablation result is wrong.** I expected the 0.05 gain floor to be manufacturing the ρ ≈ 0.99 through concordant ties at the floor, and it is not: ρ(B,D) = 0.988 over all 21 compounds, 0.981 after dropping the three null compounds, 0.979 after dropping every floor-saturated compound (n = 12). My criticism at I3 is structural, not a tie artefact, and I have stated it that way.
- **`--fast` cannot masquerade as the paper.** `results.json` records `fast: false`, `partial: false`, and S7's middle column is read from the record rather than asserted.
- **Seeding discipline.** Shuffle *i* depends only on `(seed, i)` (`nullmodels.py`), the permutation sweep genuinely re-reads prefixes of the same draws rather than re-running, and the paired landscape design is correctly described.
- **The evaluation taxonomy (§2.5) is honest.** The three-way split, the DOI/PMID-computed shared-source labelling, the refusal to repair the two rank inversions, and the flat statement that the circuit layer has **no** out-of-sample test are all genuine and go further than most papers in this area.
- **`data/literature/README.md` is exemplary.** It records ten places where the literature contradicts the shipped library — including that fipronil at `vertebrate_GABA_A` is ~9× off its own citation and ~4000× off against the β3 homopentamer — *without editing the library to match*. This is the single most trustworthy artefact in the repository and I would encourage the authors to point to it more prominently in §2.1.
- **The Discussion's encoded-versus-generated separation is sound** with the one exception noted at I15 (caveat (i)). I checked each item in the "Model-generated" list against the pipeline and each is genuinely computed.
- **The "prospective, not pre-registered" correction and the demotion of the capped-*d* sample sizes** are both done properly, and the reasons given (a mutable repository is not a registry; model-internal intervals contain no biological variance) are correct.

## Closing

The instincts behind this paper are right, and the machinery for testing one's own claims is the part worth publishing. My concern is that the machinery has so far been pointed outward rather than inward: the instruments test the predictions, but nothing tests the instruments, and when I did so, three of the four headline results turned out to depend on properties of the substrate (row normalisation, a mislabelled null, a graph that is 76% a two-hub star) rather than on the pharmacology–connectome integration. A revision that documents the engine honestly, fixes the transmitter null, repeats the analysis on a graph that has topology to speak of, regenerates the stability matrix at a shuffle count that can reject, and narrows the type-system claim to what the code does would be a genuinely good paper — and a shorter, sharper one.
